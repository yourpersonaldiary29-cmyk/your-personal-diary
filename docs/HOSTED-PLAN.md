## חלק א' – סיכום לבעלים (עברית)

### מה תקבל

- **חשבון אחד, אותו יומן, מכל מקום**: נרשמים פעם אחת בשרת של Your Personal Diary (שרת קטן בענן שאתה משלם עליו), ואז נכנסים עם אותו שם משתמש וסיסמה מהדפדפן, מאפליקציית Windows ומאפליקציית Android – בבית, בעבודה, בחו"ל. לא צריך שהמחשב בבית יהיה דלוק, לא צריך DuckDNS, לא צריך לפתוח פורטים בנתב, ובעיית ה-CGNAT של הקו הביתי לא רלוונטית יותר.
- **כל מי שירצה יוכל להירשם** (ההרשמה פתוחה לאינטרנט, עם הגנות), וכל משתמש רואה רק את היומן שלו. אתה הוא **הבעלים** – לא "מי שנרשם ראשון" אלא החשבון שמזההו רשום בהגדרת השרת (`DIARY_OWNER_ID`, צעד 7 למטה). רק אתה רואה את כרטיס הדואר האלקטרוני (לאיפוס סיסמה) ואת כרטיס "השרת" החדש (ארכיון של כל השרת, מצב הדיסק, מונה הרשמות). אם חשבון הבעלים נמחק – אף אחד אחר לא הופך לבעלים מעצמו (זה היה פרצה בגרסה 1: בשרת ציבורי "החשבון הוותיק הבא" הוא זר).
- **מי שכבר משתמש באפליקציית Windows ממשיך בדיוק כמו היום** (יומן מקומי במחשב). שום דבר לא זז בלי בחירה מפורשת שלו. מי שירצה לעבור לשרת עושה זאת עם קובץ גיבוי (יצירת גיבוי במחשב ← שחזור בחשבון שבשרת), וחזרה באותה דרך.
- **ההצפנה נשארת כמו שהיא**: כל יומן הוא קובץ `.mpd` מוצפן (AES-256-GCM) במפתח שנגזר מהסיסמה של המשתמש – ההצפנה היא של האפליקציה, לא של ספק האירוח. מה שמשתנה: הקבצים יושבים עכשיו על דיסק של ספק האירוח באמסטרדם ולא במחשב שלך, וכשמשתמש מחובר היומן שלו פתוח בזיכרון של השרת (בדיוק כפי שהיה בזיכרון של המחשב).

**ההמלצה (ובחירתך – "דרך א"): Railway, תוכנית Pro (20 $ לחודש), אזור EU West (אמסטרדם).** הסיבות: מחיר קבוע וידוע; לוח בקרה פשוט שבו גם אתה תוכל לראות "השרת רץ / לא רץ", לוגים, ולחצן גיבוי/שחזור; פריסה בלי git ובלי Docker; ו-SMTP עובד (רק ב-Pro). למה לא Hobby ב-5 $: שליחת מייל חסומה בו, והחלופה – שירות מייל בממשק HTTPS (Resend וכדומה, מה ש-Railway ממליץ) – פירושה ספק נוסף שרואה את מיילי האיפוס, כתובת יוצאת חדשה בקוד השרת, ושינוי של כלל ביקורת הרשת ושל מדיניות הפרטיות; לא נבחר. Fly.io נשאר החלופה המתועדת (טכנית שקול, מצפין את הדיסק כברירת מחדל, עובד עם DuckDNS, זול יותר) – אבל לפני מעבר אליו חובה לבדוק בפועל שחיבור ל-`smtp.gmail.com:465` נפתח מתוך המכונה. השרת לא תלוי בספק: כל ההגדרות ממשתני סביבה, מעבר = פריסה מחדש, לא שינוי קוד.

**הכתובת**: ההמלצה היא **לא** להשתמש ב-`yourpersonaldiary.duckdns.org`. DuckDNS הוא שירות חינמי ללא התחייבות, הוא לא יודע CNAME (וזה מה ש-Railway דורש), ובכל מקרה שם המתחם יופיע ביומני השקיפות של תעודות ה-HTTPS. במקום זה:
1. **להתחיל עם הכתובת שהספק נותן חינם**, למשל `https://yourpersonaldiary.up.railway.app` (כשיוצרים את הכתובת אפשר לבחור את החלק הראשון, אם הוא פנוי). היא קבועה, מקבלת תעודת HTTPS אוטומטית, ואותה נצרוב כברירת מחדל באפליקציות.
2. **אם תרצה שם יפה משלך** (למשל `yourpersonaldiary.com`): לקנות דומיין אמיתי אצל רשם דומיינים (כ-10–15 $ לשנה; לא דרך DuckDNS). רשם אמיתי יודע CNAME, ההעברה לספק אחר בעתיד לא תשנה את הכתובת שבאפליקציות, והתעודה עדיין אוטומטית. אפשר לעשות זאת גם אחרי שהכול כבר עובד.

### מה תעשה בעצמך (פעם אחת; בלי קוד, בלי הרשאות מנהל; כשעה בסך הכול, לא ברצף)

הסוכנים מכינים הכול (קוד, קובץ Docker, הגדרות) ופורסים מהטרמינל במחשב שלך. אתה עושה רק מה שדורש **זהות, כסף או סוד** שלך. כלל ברזל חדש (ממצא הסקירה): **שום סוד לא עובר דרך הסוכן** – לא בצ'אט, לא בפלט של פקודה, לא ברשימת משתנים. סוכן שמחובר ל-Railway בכניסה מלאה יכול לקרוא את כל המשתנים הלא-חתומים, להיכנס לתוך המכונה ולהוריד את כל קבצי הדיסק – לכן הסוכנים לא מקבלים כניסה מלאה אלא אסימון פרויקט (צעד 4), ואתה מתחבר בעצמך רק לפעולות שלך (צעדים 9 ו-12) ומתנתק אחריהן.

1. **חשבון Railway** (railway.com): הרשמה עם חשבון Google או מייל, בחירת תוכנית **Pro (20 $ לחודש)**, הזנת כרטיס אשראי, הפעלת אימות דו-שלבי (מי ששולט בחשבון Railway שולט בשרת ובדיסק). אם כבר נפתח Hobby – לשדרג ל-Pro **ולפרוס מחדש** אחר כך (כך כתוב בתיעוד: אחרי שדרוג חייבים פריסה מחדש כדי ש-SMTP ייפתח).
2. **הסכם עיבוד נתונים (DPA)**: Railway מציעים הסכם DPA סטנדרטי בחתימה עצמית (קישור DocuSign בדף docs.railway.com/maturity/compliance), חינם, כ-5 דקות. נדרש כי אתה "בעל השליטה" במידע של המשתמשים והספק האמריקאי מעבד אותו (סעיף הפרטיות למטה).
3. **האזור**: בהגדרות השירות (Settings ← Region) – **EU West (Amsterdam)** – **לפני** שיוצרים את הדיסק (הדיסק נצמד לאזור של השירות; העברה אחר כך = השבתה).
4. **אסימון פרויקט לסוכנים**: Project Settings ← Tokens ← יצירת אסימון לסביבת production ← להדביק אותו במשתנה סביבה של המשתמש שלך ב-Windows בשם `RAILWAY_TOKEN` (חיפוש "ערוך משתני סביבה עבור החשבון שלך" בתפריט התחל – לא דורש מנהל; הסוכן יגיד לך מה ללחוץ). הסוכנים פורסים איתו (`railway up`) ורואים לוגים; הוא לא מאפשר להם להיכנס למכונה או להוריד את הדיסק, ולעולם לא מודפס בצ'אט. **הסוכנים לא מריצים `railway login`.**
5. **מפתח השרת – אתה מייצר אותו**: בחלון PowerShell רגיל שלך מדביקים שורה אחת (הסוכן נותן אותה; גם בחלק ב' 3.2): `$b = New-Object byte[] 32; [System.Security.Cryptography.RandomNumberGenerator]::Create().GetBytes($b); [Convert]::ToBase64String($b)` – מתקבלת מחרוזת של 44 תווים. מעתיקים אותה (א) ל-Railway ← השירות ← Variables ← משתנה חדש `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` ← בתפריט ⋮ של המשתנה **Seal** (חתימה: מרגע זה אף אחד, גם לא אתה ולא ה-API, לא יכול לקרוא אותו בחזרה), ו-(ב) **באותו רגע** למנהל הסיסמאות שלך – זה העותק היחיד. סוגרים את חלון ה-PowerShell. בלי המפתח גיבוי של הדיסק לא מאפשר איפוס סיסמאות ו"זכור אותי" – אבל היומנים עצמם נפתחים תמיד בסיסמה של כל משתמש.
6. **קוד הזמנה לתקופת הפתיחה**: בוחר משפט סודי משלך ומגדיר אותו באותו מסך כמשתנה `DIARY_INVITE_CODE` (גם Seal). עד שתסיר אותו, רק מי שיודע את הקוד יכול להירשם – כך אף זר לא נרשם לפניך.
7. **ההרשמה הראשונה + מזהה הבעלים**: אחרי שהסוכן מודיע "השרת למעלה", פותחים את הכתובת בדפדפן ונרשמים עם קוד ההזמנה. אחר כך: הגדרות ← כרטיס "חשבון" ← "מזהה חשבון" (מחרוזת כמו `u_3f9a…`, לא סודית) ← מעתיקים ל-Railway ← Variables ← `DIARY_OWNER_ID` ← Deploy. רק מרגע זה כרטיסי הבעלים (דואר, שרת) נדלקים – ורק לך. בלי המשתנה אין לאף אחד כרטיסי בעלים, והשרת כותב על כך שורה בלוג.
8. **סיסמת אפליקציה ל-Gmail** (ל"שכחתי סיסמה"): בחשבון Google – אימות דו-שלבי ← "סיסמאות לאפליקציות" ← יצירה. את 16 האותיות אתה מדביק בעצמך בכרטיס "דואר אלקטרוני" בהגדרות של היומן (הכרטיס עובד מרחוק, בסיסמת החשבון שלך). היא נשמרת מוצפנת בשרת, לא בקוד, לא במשתני הסביבה ולא בצ'אט. **"שלח מייל בדיקה" חייב להגיע** – זה תנאי קבלה של הפריסה, לא רק של העלייה לאוויר.
9. **גיבויים**: (א) בלוח הבקרה של Railway, בשירות ← לשונית Backups ← להפעיל **Daily** וגם **Weekly** (שחזור בלחצן Restore). (ב) פעם בחודש, עותק שלא תלוי בספק: בהגדרות היומן ← כרטיס "השרת" ← "הכן ארכיון של השרת" (מקלידים סיסמת ארכיון ששומרים במנהל הסיסמאות) – השרת כותב קובץ מוצפן אחד על הדיסק שלו ומציג לך את פקודת ההורדה; בחלון PowerShell שלך: `railway login` (דפדפן), הפקודה שהוצגה (`railway volume files download …`), ואז `railway logout`. את הקובץ שומרים בכונן חיצוני/ענן משלך. הארכיון **לא** ניתן להורדה דרך האתר – בכוונה (חלק ב' 3.3).
10. **בדיקה מהטלפון בנתוני סלולר** (לא Wi-Fi ביתי): הרשמה של חשבון ניסיון, רשומה, "שכחתי סיסמה" (המייל מגיע), מחיקת חשבון הניסיון; ואז באפליקציית Windows: בחירת "השרת" והתחברות. רק אחרי זה מפרסמים את הכתובת באפליקציות (עדכון גרסה).
11. **ניטור (מומלץ מאוד)**: חשבון חינמי בשירות "uptime monitor" שבודק את `https://<הכתובת>/api/health/strict` כל 5 דקות ושולח לך מייל כשהשרת לא עונה **או כשהדיסק מלא / כמעט מלא** (הכתובת הזו עונה "שגיאה" גם אז). זהו האזעקה האמיתית: Railway בודק את השרת רק בזמן פריסה ולא אחר כך.

### מה משתנה בפרטיות (וחייב להיכתב במדיניות הפרטיות)

היום המדיניות אומרת "היומן שמור רק במכשיר שלך; אנחנו לא מקבלים כלום". **למשתמשי השרת זה כבר לא נכון**, ואלה המשפטים שישתנו (בגרסה המלאה – חלק ב', סעיף 6):

(בגרסה 1 נכתב "באיחוד האירופי" – זה לא מדויק מבחינה משפטית כשהספק אמריקאי.)
2. **מי מצפין**: "קבצי היומן מוצפנים על ידי האפליקציה עצמה (AES-256-GCM, מפתח שנגזר מהסיסמה שלך); הספק לא מתחייב להצפנת דיסק משלו." (Railway לא מתעד הצפנה במנוחה; Fly כן – המשפט על הספק ישתנה רק אם עוברים ל-Fly.)
3. **מה אנחנו (אתה) יכולים לראות**: "רשימת החשבונות (שמות, שמות משתמש, כתובות מייל, זמני הרשמה וכניסה) אינה מוצפנת וגלויה למפעיל השרת. תוכן היומן מוצפן בקובץ במפתח שנגזר מהסיסמה שלך; בזמן שאתה מחובר הוא מפוענח בזיכרון השרת."
4. **מפתח השחזור – להגיד את זה בפה מלא**: כדי ש"שכחתי סיסמה" יעבוד, השרת שומר לכל חשבון עותק של מפתח היומן, עטוף במפתח של השרת. פירוש הדבר: **מפעיל השרת (אתה) יכול טכנית לאפס סיסמה של כל חשבון – ולכן גם לקרוא כל יומן – בלי לדעת את הסיסמה.** זה בדיוק מה שהמדיניות הנוכחית אומרת על "מי שיש לו גישה מלאה למחשב" (סעיף 6 במדיניות), רק שעכשיו "המחשב" הוא השרת שלך. המדיניות תגיד: "המפעיל מתחייב לא לעשות זאת אלא לבקשת בעל החשבון; אין לנו צורך בכך ואין לנו ממשק לכך; מי שאינו מוכן לסמוך על המפעיל – ישתמש במצב 'רק במכשיר הזה'." הצעד הטכני שמצמצם את הסיכון (חלק ב' 3.2): מפתח השרת הוא "סוד חתום" בלוח הבקרה, שאתה יצרת בעצמך, לא קובץ על הדיסק – כך גיבוי של הדיסק לבדו לא פותח יומנים, וגם סוכן שפורס את השרת לא רואה אותו.
5. **מחיקה**: "מחיקת חשבון מוחקת מיד את היומן ואת הרשומה מהשרת. אם איבדת גישה, אפשר לבקש מחיקה במייל מהכתובת הרשומה בחשבון ואנו נבצע אותה ידנית. עותקים בגיבויי הדיסק של ספק האירוח נמחקים מעצמם בתום תקופת השמירה (עד 89 יום לגיבוי חודשי); הארכיון המוצפן של המפעיל מתחלף בחודש שאחריו."
6. **לוגים**: "השרת כותב לוג טכני ללא תוכן יומן, ללא כתובות מייל וללא כתובות IP של משתמשים."

### מה קורה לאפליקציות

- **דפדפן**: הכתובת של השרת היא האתר. אותו ממשק בדיוק. אפשר "להוסיף למסך הבית" בטלפון.
- **Windows**: במסך הראשון (רק במחשב שעוד אין בו יומן) – "איפה היומן שלך? ○ במחשב הזה ○ בשרת Your Personal Diary". הבחירה נזכרת במחשב ואפשר לשנות אותה בהגדרות (כרטיס "איפה היומן שלך"). במצב "שרת" השרת המקומי בכלל לא מופעל. מי שכבר יש לו יומן במחשב – לא רואה את המסך, וממשיך כרגיל.
- **Android**: אחרי מסך בחירת השפה – אותה שאלה, עם "השרת" מסומן כברירת מחדל (הכתובת צרובה באפליקציה, במקום אחד). "רק בטלפון הזה" = מה שנבנה עד היום. "דילוג – רק להסתכל" (סיור) נשאר במצב "בטלפון הזה"; במצב "שרת" הסיור לא מוצע בגרסה הראשונה (הסבר בחלק ב' 4c).
- **מעבר של יומן קיים**: במחשב – הגדרות ← גיבוי ← "צור קובץ גיבוי". אחר כך בוחרים "השרת", נרשמים, ובהגדרות ← שחזור בוחרים את הקובץ ומקלידים את סיסמת הגיבוי. היומן מוחלף (לא ממוזג) – לכן עושים זאת לחשבון חדש וריק. חזרה למחשב – אותה דרך הפוכה.
- **גבולות בשרת** (חדש, כדי שאף משתמש לא ימלא את הדיסק או את הזיכרון): יומן עד 10 MB (כ-14,000 רשומות טקסט; שחזור מקובץ גדול יותר נדחה עם הודעה), עד 300 חשבונות (ניתן להגדיל יחד עם הדיסק), הרשמה מוגבלת לפי כתובת, ונעילה אחרי 10 סיסמאות שגויות שמותאמת לשרת ציבורי (זר לא יכול לנעול אותך בכוונה – חלק ב' 5.2).

## Part B – Technical design (English, binding)

Conventions: file paths are relative to the repository root. ### 1. Hosting platform for a non-programmer owner

#### 1.1 What the server needs from a host

From the code, the hosted server is: one Node.js process (`server.cjs`, esbuild bundle, `target: 'node22'`, `format: 'cjs'` – `scripts/build-server.mjs`), no native modules (SQLite is `sql.js` WebAssembly, PDF is `pdfkit` + bundled TTF fonts, crypto is `node:crypto`), one HTTP listener, and one writable directory that must support atomic `rename` and `fsync` (`writeContainerAtomic` in `server/src/security/vault.ts`: "tmp -> fsync -> current becomes .prev -> tmp becomes current"; `writeJsonAtomic` in `server/src/security/accounts.ts`). That rules out object storage and network file shares as the data directory and asks for a block volume mounted into the container. The process keeps state in memory (sessions, open vaults, lockout counters), so exactly one instance may run at a time (section 3.8).

#### 1.3 Recommendation

**Railway, Pro plan ($20/month), region EU West (Amsterdam), one service, one volume (10 GB provisioned, billed by use) mounted at `/data`.** This is the owner's decision ("דרך א" = option (a) of the critical review finding). Rationale, in order of weight for this owner: (1) a fixed, predictable price; (2) the owner-facing dashboard covers everything he will ever need to see or press (service up/down, logs, metrics, scheduled backups with a Restore button); (3) `railway up` deploys the working directory without git or Docker; (4) no sleep/HA defaults to defuse; (5) SMTP works on Pro, so the existing Mail card and reset flow run unchanged.

Why not the cheaper options the review listed: **(b) Fly.io as primary** (~$5) is technically equivalent and its docs do not document an SMTP block, but that is an absence of documentation, not a promise; it also needs the fly.toml overrides of 1.4 and CLI-only snapshot restores. It stays the documented fallback, with one hard precondition: before any move, open a TLS connection to `smtp.gmail.com:465` from `fly ssh console` and send a test mail through the Mail card on a staging app. **(c) an HTTPS mail API** (Resend/SendGrid/Mailgun/Postmark – Railway's own recommendation for Hobby) would keep the $5 plan but means a second processor that sees every reset mail and address, a new outbound host and transport in `server/src/mail/`, a change to the audit rule of 5.8 (`scripts/check-no-network.mjs` would have to allow-list that API host), and a new provider in the privacy policy. Rejected for v1; it remains the escape hatch if Gmail SMTP ever becomes unusable. **Nothing in the server depends on the platform**: every hosted-mode input is an environment variable (section 2.1), so moving from Railway to Fly.io is a redeploy, not a code change.

Domain advice: do **not** use `yourpersonaldiary.duckdns.org`. Railway cannot serve it (CNAME required), DuckDNS is a best-effort free service with no availability promise, and the name is public anyway (certificate transparency – the current privacy policy already says so). Use the provider's own hostname (chosen prefix, e.g. `https://yourpersonaldiary.up.railway.app`) as `DIARY_PUBLIC_URL` and as the default baked into the apps. If the owner wants a memorable name, buy a real domain (~10–15 USD/year) at a registrar that supports CNAME; it keeps the app-side address stable across providers. Either way there is exactly one public hostname; the server's Host check (section 2.3) accepts only that one.

### 2. Server "hosted mode" (`server/src`)

#### 2.2 Behind the platform's TLS proxy

- **Client IP**: the limiter keys on `req.socket.remoteAddress` today (`rateLimit` in `server/src/middleware/access.ts`: `const key = req.socket.remoteAddress || 'unknown'`) and forgot-password passes `ip: req.socket.remoteAddress || 'unknown'` (`server/src/routes/auth.ts`). Behind a proxy every request would share the proxy's address. Hosted mode introduces `clientIp(req)`: with `DIARY_CLIENT_IP_HEADER=x-forwarded-for` it is `req.ip` (Express' proxy-addr with `trust proxy` = `TRUST_PROXY`, i.e. the address the configured hop count selects; local mode never sets `trust proxy`, so there `req.ip === socket.remoteAddress` and nothing changes), with a single-value header name it is that header (Fly's `Fly-Client-IP`), unset it is the socket address. **The hop count is not documented by Railway** (neither docs.railway.com/guides/public-networking nor /reference/public-networking mentions `X-Forwarded-For`), so it is an empirical fact to establish, not an assumption: WP-H4 mounts, on staging only (`DIARY_DEBUG_ROUTES=1`), `GET /api/debug/proxy` → `{ ip, ips, xff, xfp, secure, host }` and the acceptance is (i) calls from two different networks (home line, phone on mobile data) show two distinct `ip` values equal to those networks' public addresses, (ii) a call carrying a forged `X-Forwarded-For: 203.0.113.9` still shows the real address (the edge appends, and the selected hop is the edge's), (iii) `secure` is `true` and a forged `X-Forwarded-Proto: http` does not change it. If (i) fails (one shared address = the edge is more hops away), `TRUST_PROXY` is corrected and the test repeated; no go-live while a per-IP key is collapsed, because then `rateLimit(30)` on `/api/auth` would be one global bucket (finding 8). **Limiter keys bucket addresses** (finding 9): `limiterKey(ip)` = the /64 prefix for IPv6, the address itself (/32) for IPv4 – used by `rateLimit`, the register limiter and the hosted login lock (5.2), so a client holding a /64 cannot rotate past the windows.
- **HTTPS detection**: `isRemoteRequest()` is `socket.encrypted === true` (`access.ts`). Add `isSecureRequest(req) = isRemoteRequest(req) || (hosted && lastForwardedProto(req) === 'https')` – the **last** comma-separated value of `X-Forwarded-Proto` (the hop the edge wrote), not Express' `req.protocol`, which takes the leftmost value and is only safe if the edge overwrites the header. It drives HSTS (`securityHeaders` sets `Strict-Transport-Security` only for secure requests), `ServerInfo.secure`, and the reset-link base. **No "421 on plain HTTP" rule** (finding 3): the platform's health check is a direct, un-proxied request to the container (Railway: hostname `healthcheck.railway.app` on `PORT`; Fly `[[http_service.checks]]` likewise), so a secure-only rule would fail every deployment. HTTP→HTTPS is the edge's job (Railway always; Fly `force_https = true`) and HSTS keeps browsers on HTTPS afterwards; a misconfigured edge is caught by the WP-H4 header check (`curl -I https://<host>/` shows HSTS; `http://` is answered by a redirect from the edge).
- **`lanGuard` must not run in hosted mode** (finding 2): `createApp` installs `lanGuard(() => deps.getServerSettings().lanEnabled)` unconditionally (`app.ts` line 80) and the guard passes only `req.isLocal || isLanEnabled() || isRemoteRequest(req)` (`access.ts` line 65) – all three are false behind a TLS-terminating proxy with `req.isLocal = false`, so every request would get `403 server.error.lanDisabled`. `createApp` gains an optional `deps.hosted?: { publicHost: string; trustProxy: number; clientIpHeader?: string }`; when present it sets `trust proxy`, installs `hostedHostCheck` and **skips `lanGuard`** (one `if`; the local composition passes nothing and is byte-for-byte unchanged). `getServerSettings()` returns `{ port, lanEnabled: false }` and `isLanOwner: false` for everyone; the LAN card is rendered only `desktop && <LanAccessSection />` inside `PrivacySection.tsx` and is hidden for `info.mode === 'hosted'` in WP-H5. Test (`hosted-composition.test.ts`): `GET /api/server/info` over a plain socket with the public Host → 200.
- **Host check**: `hostCheck()` (`server/src/middleware/host-check.ts`) accepts localhost, LAN/loopback IP literals, the computer's own names and the remote hostname. Hosted mode installs `hostedHostCheck(publicHost)` instead: allowed when `hostOf(req.headers.host) === publicHost`; **`GET /api/health` is exempt** (platform health checks address the container directly, not through the public name); everything else `421`, same body as today. `host-check.ts` imports `node:os`/`node:net` and is desktop-only by design ("only server.ts - the desktop composition - imports it, never a route"); the hosted check is a small pure function next to it.
- **Security headers served by us**: `securityHeaders` already sends `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY` and a same-origin CSP (`default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; ... connect-src 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'`). Hosted additions: HSTS `max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains` on every secure answer (today only on the HTTPS listener), `Cache-Control: no-store` on `/api/*`, and `Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=()`. The web client is same-origin, so the CSP needs no change.
- **CORS allow-list**: today `ALLOWED_ORIGINS = new Set(['tauri://localhost', 'http://tauri.localhost', 'https://tauri.localhost'])`. Evidence for the desktop origin on Windows: Tauri's `useHttpsScheme` "Sets whether the custom protocols should use `https://<scheme>.localhost` instead of the default `http://<scheme>.localhost` ... on Windows and Android. Defaults to `false`" (v2.tauri.app/reference/config). The app does not set it (`src-tauri/tauri.conf.json` has no `useHttpsScheme`), so the Windows origin is `http://tauri.localhost`; the set already contains it. Android: Capacitor `server.hostname` "Default: `localhost`" and `server.androidScheme` "Default: `https`" (capacitorjs.com/docs/config); `capacitor.config.ts` sets `androidScheme: 'https'` and leaves the hostname → origin **`https://localhost`**. Hosted mode's set: the three Tauri origins + `https://localhost` (+ `capacitor://localhost` reserved for a future iOS build). `DEV_ORIGINS` stay off unless `--dev`. Allowing `https://localhost` is safe because authentication is a bearer token the page must already hold (`credentials: 'omit'` in `src/api/client.ts`); CORS only decides whether a page may read the answer. Preflight: `cors()` already answers `OPTIONS` with 204 and `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: Authorization, Content-Type, Accept-Language`.
- **Rate limits per real address**: limits keyed by `limiterKey(clientIp(req))`: `/api/auth` **10/min per bucket** in hosted mode (`DIARY_LOGIN_PER_MINUTE`; 30 stays local), backup 20/min, mail test 10/min, plus a **global** `/api/auth` cap of 300/min (`DIARY_AUTH_GLOBAL_PER_MINUTE`): even if per-address keying is ever defeated (rotated addresses, a collapsed key) the CPU-bearing endpoints are bounded by it and by the scrypt gate (5.4). New: the register limiter (5 per hour and 20 per day per bucket – 2.6). The `hits` map already evicts idle addresses when it exceeds 1000 entries.
- **Login lockout on a public server** (finding 12): `login()` keys the counter on `${account ? account:<id> : name:<username>}${channel}` with `channel = opts.fromNetwork ? '@network' : ''` (`auth-service.ts` lines 568–569) – one counter per account for the whole internet, 10 failures → 15 minutes **for everyone, including the right password**, so ten wrong guesses every 15 minutes from anywhere keep any chosen user (the owner included) out indefinitely. Hosted mode passes `{ fromNetwork: true, bucket: limiterKey(clientIp) }` and the lock key becomes `account:<id>@<bucket>` (the hard 10 → 15 min lock applies to the attacker's /64 or /32 only); a second, purely per-account counter (`account:<id>@any`) adds a **progressive delay** instead of a lock: after 10 failures within 15 minutes each further attempt on that account waits `min(2^(n−10), 30)` seconds (a `setTimeout`, no CPU, connections bounded by the limiters) before scrypt runs – the owner's worst case is a 30 s login, never a lockout. Unknown names are counted the same way (`name:<username>@<bucket>`), so nothing leaks. Remembered sessions are tokens, not logins, and are never affected; the confirm-step lock (`CONFIRM_LOCKOUT_PREFIX`) stays per account because abusing it needs a stolen token. Owner recovery if ever needed: `admin.cjs unlock` (3.10). Local mode: unchanged (no bucket → today's keys).

#### 2.3 What "local request" means now

`markLocal` sets `req.isLocal = isLoopbackAddress(req.socket.remoteAddress)`. In hosted mode the platform's proxy connects from a private address, never loopback, but to make the rule explicit `markLocal` sets `req.isLocal = false` whenever `config.mode === 'hosted'`. Consequences, all intended:

- `GET /api/auth/accounts` → `403` (`ACCOUNTS_LOCAL_ONLY_KEY`): the public server never lists accounts. The login page already handles this (`LoginPage.tsx`: `if (!info || info.isLocalRequest !== true) return []`).
- `POST /api/auth/guest` → not mounted (section 4c).
- `destPath` / `srcPath` → hosted mode mounts `refuseDiskPaths` before the backup/export routers, so a path never reaches `fs` (the routes themselves already demand `req.isLocal` for paths: `if (!req.isLocal) throw ApiError.forbidden('server.backup.saveLocalOnly')`; the 400 is friendlier than that 403 and identical to the phone). **Location** (finding 14): the middleware moves from `mobile/core/app.ts` (lines 67–73; that file imports `./express-shim` on line 40 and the whole route set, which esbuild would drag into the server bundle) to a new `server/src/middleware/disk-paths.ts` with no imports beyond `../errors`; `mobile/core/app.ts` switches to importing it from there (a one-line change in WP-H3, re-read before editing; if the mobile tree is being edited by the Android agents at that moment, leave its local copy and report it in openIssues – ten duplicated lines are acceptable).
- `GET /api/settings/data-dir` → `dataDir: ''`, `dbPath: ''` (already: `req.isLocal ? ... : ''`).
- `GET /api/server/info` → `dataDir: ''`, `lanUrls: []`, `remoteUrl: DIARY_PUBLIC_URL`, `secure: true`, `isLocalRequest: false`, plus new optional fields `mode: 'hosted'`, `publicUrl`, `storageOk`, `registration: 'open'|'invite'|'closed'`.

**Owner authorisation from anywhere – an explicit owner (finding 6).** Today the owner is the first real account (`firstAccountId` in `server/src/security/owner.ts`) and a stored owner whose account is gone is released and replaced by the next-oldest one: `ownerOnceReleased = (stored) => (stored && accountExists(stored) ? stored : firstAccount())` (`routes/server.ts` line 197), used by `mailGate` (line 351) and `visibleSettings`; `delete-account` releases the mail owner (`routes/auth.ts` lines 369–377). On a public server "the next-oldest account" is a stranger who would inherit the Mail card and the server archive the moment the owner deletes his account, re-registers, or a platform backup from before his registration is restored. Hosted mode therefore does **not** use that rule: the owner is `DIARY_OWNER_ID` and nothing else. Concretely, `serverRouter` gets `deps.ownerId?: () => string` (hosted: the env value; local: undefined → today's code path untouched). When defined: `mailGate`, the Server card (`/api/server/status`, `/api/server/archive`) and `visibleSettings` compare the signed-in id with `ownerId()`; `ownerId() === ''` or no account with that id → `403 server.owner.unset` for everyone, with one log line at start and on each refusal (rate-limited to once a minute); `isMailOwner`/`isLanOwner`/`isRemoteOwner` are `false` for everyone else, so the cards never render. `pinUnownedSettings`/`releaseOwner` keep running (they only clear secrets of a deleted owner – desirable). The flow: the owner registers with the invite code, reads his **Account ID** in Settings → Account (new read-only line; `GET /api/auth/me` already returns `user.id` – `UserProfile.id`, `shared/types.ts` line 50), and sets the variable (Part A step 7). Hosted tests: (a) `DIARY_OWNER_ID` unset → owner routes 403 for the first account too; (b) owner set → 200 for that id, 403 for a second account; (c) **the owner deletes his account → the oldest remaining account still gets 403** and the mail secret has been cleared; (d) a stale id (restored backup without that account) → 403, log line. The remaining gates stay: a secure request **and**, for every `PUT`, the account password (already required: `if (typeof password !== 'string' || password === '') throw ApiError.validation(MAIL_PASSWORD_REQUIRED_KEY, 'password')`, checked with `deps.auth.verifyPassword` which is covered by the confirmation lockout `CONFIRM_LOCKOUT_PREFIX`). Reads of the Mail card (`GET /api/server/mail`) return no secret (`MailSettingsView` has `smtpPasswordSet`, never the password). A second factor (TOTP) for the owner stays a later package (WP-H10): with the archive no longer downloadable through the API (3.3), the owner password alone unlocks nothing that reads diaries. `PUT /api/server/settings` (LAN switch) stays local-only – it has no meaning on the server and answers `403 server.server.settingsLocalOnly` as today.

#### 2.6 Registration policy – decision

Registration is **open to the internet** (the owner's goal), protected by: the register limiter – 5 per hour and 20 per day **per address bucket** (/64 for IPv6, /32 for IPv4; counted on every attempt, successful or not); `DIARY_MAX_ACCOUNTS` as the hard ceiling; **no global daily cap** (finding 9: a hard 200/day would let one /64 holder block all registrations for a day and, with the account cap, forever – instead a 24 h registration counter is shown in the Server card and logged with a warning above 50/day, and the invite switch is the real brake); the per-account diary quota; the existing password policy (`passwordProblemKey`), e-mail/username uniqueness and the 100 KB body limit; and the `invite` switch for the launch window (so the owner is guaranteed to be the first account) or for pausing sign-ups. **E-mail verification is not required in v1.** Reasons: (a) the server sends mail only when the owner configured SMTP, so verification cannot be a precondition of registering; (b) the existing reset flow already proves mailbox access when it matters (`forgot-password`), and an account registered with somebody else's address cannot be taken over by its registrant through mail – only by the mailbox owner; (c) the cost is a whole new state machine (pending accounts, expiry, re-send) for little gain on a diary. The one real downside is kept: `409 VALIDATION field: "email"` on `register` tells a caller that an address is registered. In local mode this is harmless; on the internet it is a (rate-limited: 5 registrations per hour per IP) enumeration oracle. Accepted and documented in section 5.3; `forgot-password` stays neutral ("תמיד אותה תשובה"). If abuse appears, WP-H10 adds "verify your e-mail to enable password reset" (soft verification: `emailVerifiedAt` on the account; reset mails only to verified addresses).

#### 2.7 Multi-user isolation review (verified)

- Every data router is mounted behind `requireAuth(deps.auth)` (`app.ts` lines 95–107) and handlers reach data only through `ctxOf(req)` → `{ session, account, user, vault, db }` built by `authenticate()` from the bearer token. Each account has its **own SQLite database** in its own encrypted file (`users/<id>.mpd`, `vaultPathOf`), so there is no shared table in which a row could belong to someone else – entry and habit ids are per database.
- Session listing/revocation is scoped: `deps.auth.listSessions(ctx.account.id)` and `revokeSession(ctx.account.id, id)` (`routes/auth.ts`).
- Backup restore swaps only `ctx.vault` (`replaceDatabase`); export reads `ctx.db`.
- Owner routes: `ownerGate` / `mailGate` compare the signed-in id with the stored owner or the first account; a guest is never an owner.
- Public endpoints expose: `/api/health` (ok, version), `/api/server/info` (version, port, startedAt, flags; `dataDir` blank for non-local). No account data.
- Cross-account files: account deletion only touches `pendingDeletes` names of the form `<name>.mpd` under `users/` ("שלעולם לא נוגע ביומן של חשבון קיים").
- Residual: the recovery key (`recoveryWrappedKey`) of every account is wrapped with the one device key, so isolation **at the file level** between accounts is only as strong as the operator (section 3.2, 5.1). Isolation **through the API** is complete.

#### 2.8 Body limits, upload abuse, disk-full, logging

- Limits stay: `SMALL_BODY_LIMIT = '100kb'`, `DATA_BODY_LIMIT = '2mb'`, `BACKUP_BODY_LIMIT = '25mb'` with `BACKUP_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE = 20`; bodies are parsed only after the session check ("a request without a valid session is answered before its body is read"). Restore of a crafted file is bounded: `MAX_UNTRUSTED_KDF_COST = 2 * DEFAULT_KDF.N * DEFAULT_KDF.r * DEFAULT_KDF.p` (`kdf.ts`) refuses expensive headers; the image is then opened by sql.js (WebAssembly sandbox) and `integrity_check`-ed before it replaces anything.
- New: per-account quota (`DIARY_MAX_DIARY_BYTES` = 10 MB) checked in `persist()` against the encrypted size and in restore against the uploaded size (`413 server.backup.tooLarge` before the file is decrypted); account cap (300); register limiter.
- Disk full (finding 15): `persist` already retries with back-off ("Keep trying with back-off; the data is still safe in memory"; `RETRY_DELAY_MS = 5_000`, capped at 60 s – `vault.ts` lines 32, 174) and the client gets no error for debounced writes. But other writes fail loudly: `sessions.ts` `write()` throws `server.auth.sessionsFileUnwritable` (line 185), so a login with "remember me" would answer **500** (`create()` persists on line 239), and `accounts.ts` `writeJsonAtomic` makes registration and password change fail. Hosted mode: (1) `/api/health` stays HTTP 200 with `"storage":"degraded"` (a 503 would make the platform recycle a healthy process and lose nothing but time – and Railway does not even monitor it after deploy), **and** a second path `/api/health/strict` answers **503** when storage is degraded or free space < 10 % – that is the path the external uptime monitor polls (3.6), so disk-full reaches the owner's inbox within minutes; (2) a remember-me login whose sessions-file write fails is **downgraded to a non-remembered session** (`AuthResponse.remembered: false`, the client shows "Signed in for this session only – the server's storage is full") instead of 500; (3) registration / password change / profile change on `ENOSPC` answer `507 server.error.storageFull` with a clear text instead of a generic 500; (4) a warning line in the log once per state change and the Server card's banner. Volume growth is a dashboard action (Railway: "live resize with zero downtime"; Fly: `fly volumes extend`).
- Logging without personal data: logs go to stdout/stderr (the platform collects them; there is no `server.log` file – that is written by the desktop shell, `lib.rs`). Rules: no IP addresses, no e-mail addresses, no usernames, no Host header values, no tokens; account ids (opaque `u_…`) are allowed as today (`persist failed for ${this.userId}`); counters instead of identities for limiter events ("register refused (rate): 3 in the last minute"). The 29 existing `console.*` sites were grepped: none prints an address or an e-mail. The `listening` event keeps `dataDir` (`/data`, not personal).

#### 2.9 Keeping local mode identical

- All changes are additive and gated: `config.mode`, new middleware installed only in `hosted.ts`, new env reads inside `if (hosted)`.
- `server.ts` (desktop composition) changes in exactly two places: it passes `mode` through and uses `clientIp()` (which equals `socket.remoteAddress` when `trust proxy` is false and no header is configured). `app.ts` changes only inside `if (deps.hosted)` branches (trust proxy, host check, no `lanGuard`, the debug routes) – without `deps.hosted` the middleware chain is identical to today's, which a snapshot test of the chain's names asserts.
- Gate: the existing suites (`server/test/**`, including `host-check-listeners`, `remote-host-check`, `remote-lockout`, `lan-owner`, `desktop-no-guest`) must pass unchanged; `node scripts/build-server.mjs` and its smoke test unchanged; the desktop `dist` contains no server-mode code paths other than the client-side "Where is your diary?" feature (section 4b), which is behind a stored choice and never shows for existing installs.

### 3. Data and operations

#### 3.1 Data directory on the volume

`/data` (= `DIARY_DATA_DIR`), same layout as `server/src/config.ts` documents: `accounts.json` (registry: names, e-mails, scrypt salt/params, verifier, `recoveryWrappedKey`), `server-settings.json` (only the `mail` block matters; `remote` is never written), `resets.json`, `sessions.json` (remember-me sessions: token hashes + diary keys wrapped with the device key), `users/<id>.mpd` + `.prev` + `.tmp`, and `device.key` **only when `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` is not set** (3.2). No `tls/` (remote access off). Files are created `0o600` (`writeJsonAtomic`, `writeContainerAtomic`) – meaningful on Linux, unlike Windows. The Dockerfile runs the process as a dedicated non-root user and makes `/data` writable by it at container start (an `entrypoint.sh` `chown` guarded by `id -u`, because platform volumes are typically mounted root-owned).

#### 3.2 The recovery-key escrow when the operator is the owner – state it plainly

Today: `recoveryWrappedKey` = the diary key wrapped with `device.key` (`AAD mpd-recovery-key-v1:<accountId>`), and ARCHITECTURE.md 5.1 already states the trade-off: "מי שיש לו גישה מלאה לקבצים במחשב (גם `device.key` וגם `accounts.json` + `users/<id>.mpd`) יכול לפתוח את היומן בלי הסיסמה". On a hosted server the party with full file access is **the owner-operator** (and, through the volume and its backups, the hosting provider). Therefore the privacy policy must say, in plain words, that the operator can reset any account's password and thereby read its diary, and that the only protection is the operator's honesty plus the measures below. Two technical measures, both in scope:

1. **Device key as a sealed platform secret that only the owner ever sees** (finding 7). Version 1 had the agent generate it with `node -e …` and set it with `railway variable set` – the value would have been in the agent's tool output and on a command line, contradicting "no token in the chat". Now: the owner runs, in his own Windows PowerShell 5.1 window, `$b = New-Object byte[] 32; [System.Security.Cryptography.RandomNumberGenerator]::Create().GetBytes($b); [Convert]::ToBase64String($b)` (verified on this PC: 44 characters, PowerShell 5.1.26100), pastes the result into Railway → service → Variables → `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` → ⋮ → **Seal** and into his password manager, then closes the window. Sealed: "its value is provided to builds and deployments" but "is never visible in the UI nor can it be retrieved via the API", "Sealed variable values are not provided when using `railway variables` or `railway run` via the CLI", "Sealed variables cannot be un-sealed" (docs.railway.com/guides/variables). On Fly: `fly secrets set DIARY_DEVICE_KEY=<value>` typed by the owner in his own window (Fly secrets are write-only too). `loadOrCreateDeviceKey(file)` gets an optional `override` buffer (base64-decoded, exactly 32 bytes or the server refuses to start). Effect: a copy of the volume or of a backup (accounts.json + sessions.json + .mpd) **cannot** open any diary – the wrapping key is not on the disk, and no agent or CLI session can read it back. Loss of the secret loses password reset and remember-me (users sign in again with their passwords; `ensureRecoveryKey` re-creates the escrow on the next login with a new key), never the diaries.
2. **No operator UI** for resets: the server exposes nothing that unwraps a recovery key except the user-driven reset flow (`resetWithToken` / `resetWithCode`), which needs the mail link or code sent to the account's address. The admin tool of 3.10 deletes, revokes and unlocks; it never unwraps a key. The owner would have to copy files and write code to abuse it; the policy says he will not.
3. **Agents and the platform account**: a logged-in `railway` CLI can list every unsealed variable, open a shell in the container (`railway ssh`) and download volume files (`railway volume files download`) – i.e. read all of `/data`. Agents therefore work with a project token (1.4), which deploys and reads logs; the owner alone logs in, for 3.3 and 3.10, and logs out afterwards. Part A says so in plain words.

#### 3.3 Backups – platform snapshots vs an in-app archive (recommendation)

Three layers, simplest safe thing first:

1. **Platform scheduled backups** (owner clicks once): Railway Daily (kept 6 days) + Weekly (27 days) + Monthly (89 days); restore is a dashboard button that mounts the restored volume in place of the old one. On Fly: `snapshot_retention = 30` and the restore commands of 1.4. Snapshots capture the files mid-flight only between atomic renames, which is safe: a `.mpd.tmp` is never the authoritative file and `readAndDecrypt` falls back over `[dbPath, .tmp, .prev]`.
2. **Owner-pulled encrypted server archive** (new, WP-H8; redesigned after finding 13). Version 1 streamed the archive from `GET /api/server/archive` – a GET cannot carry the password in a body, and worse, the owner's single password would have become the key to every user's data through the public API. Now the archive **never leaves the server through the API**: `POST /api/server/archive { password, passphrase }` (owner by `DIARY_OWNER_ID`, secure, 1 per 10 minutes, passphrase ≥ 12 characters) writes `/data/archive/your-personal-diary-server-<date>.mpda` – a tar of `/data` minus `archive/` and minus `device.key` (there is none when the secret is used), encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived (scrypt, same KDF) from the passphrase – keeps the previous archive as well (two files at most), and answers with the size, the SHA-256 and the exact download command. The owner then runs, in his own window, `railway login`, `railway volume files download /archive/<name>.mpda .\<name>.mpda` ("Download from a volume" – docs.railway.com/reference/cli-api) and `railway logout`; the card shows the SHA-256 so he can compare (`Get-FileHash`). Preconditions: free space ≥ 1.2 × the data size, else `507 server.archive.noSpace` ("grow the volume"). Why this shape: an attacker who holds only the owner's password can make the server write an encrypted file he cannot fetch; one who holds only the Railway account could fetch the raw volume anyway (and the sealed key is not on it) – two independent credentials are needed to get a readable whole, which is why TOTP is not pulled into v1. Everything inside is already encrypted per account except `accounts.json`/`sessions.json`/`resets.json` metadata – hence the outer encryption. The card reminds the owner when the last archive is older than 30 days. The exact CLI path semantics (`/` = volume root, as `railway volume browse /` suggests) are verified in WP-H4; if directory paths turn out unsupported, the fallback is the HTTP download `POST /api/server/archive/download` enabled only while the owner sets `DIARY_ARCHIVE_DOWNLOAD=on` in the dashboard for the download window (a variable change redeploys – acceptable monthly). This copy lives wherever the owner puts it and does not depend on the provider.
3. **Per-user backups** as today (download `.mpd`, restore with the password) – users own their data regardless of the operator.

Why not an automatic push to the owner's cloud drive: the server's network promise. Hosted mode adds **no outbound hosts except the configured SMTP** (section 5.8); mailing a weekly archive through SMTP is technically allowed and is noted as an option (Gmail attachment limits apply) but is not in v1 – layer 1 already automates retention, layer 2 gives the provider-independent copy.

#### 3.4 Restore drill (to be executed once before go-live, WP-H8 acceptance)

1. Platform restore: create a throw-away account, write an entry, take/choose a backup newer than the entry, delete the entry, restore the backup from the dashboard, redeploy, sign in: the entry is back; other accounts untouched. Record the time it took.
2. Archive restore: prepare and download the archive (3.3; the owner runs the download), then on a fresh local checkout run `node scripts/hosted/restore-archive.mjs <file> <target dir>` (it prompts for the passphrase – never an argument, so it is not in the shell history); start the server locally against that directory in hosted mode on `127.0.0.1` with `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` **typed by the owner into that window's environment** (the agent does not see it), sign in with the test account. This proves the archive is complete and the secret is correct. Without the key the server still starts and diaries open with their passwords – that is proven too (remember-me and reset are then unavailable, as designed).
3. Per-user: restore a `.mpd` backup into a second account – existing feature, run once from the phone.

#### 3.5 Upgrades and data migration

`railway up` (or `fly deploy`) builds a new image and swaps the container; with a volume this is a few seconds of downtime (both providers say so). Shutdown is graceful **only if the platform waits** (finding 4): the platform sends `SIGTERM`; `index.ts` calls `running.stop()` which awaits `remote.shutdown()` (no-op stub), closes the listener, waits up to 3 s for mail hand-over and runs `auth.shutdown()` → `vaults.closeAll()` + `sessions.close()` (`server.ts` lines 241–251, `auth-service.ts` lines 1164–1176; `sessions.close()` flushes a pending touch). Railway: "Once the new deployment is online, the old deployment is sent a SIGTERM signal" and "By default, it is given 0 seconds to gracefully shutdown before being forcefully stopped with a SIGKILL" (docs.railway.com/reference/deployments) – at 0 s none of this runs, and what is lost is not only the 250 ms debounce but every write sitting in the back-off retry (`RETRY_DELAY_MS = 5_000`, up to 60 s) and the 5 s sessions touch timer (`TOUCH_PERSIST_DELAY_MS`). Therefore **`RAILWAY_DEPLOYMENT_DRAINING_SECONDS=30`** is a mandatory service variable (1.4), `RAILWAY_DEPLOYMENT_OVERLAP_SECONDS` stays 0 (a volume cannot be mounted by two deployments), and on Fly `kill_signal = "SIGTERM"`, `kill_timeout = "20s"`. The hosted shutdown is bounded: the mail wait is 3 s, `closeAll` flushes vaults in parallel, and a vault whose write is in back-off is flushed once more immediately (`strict`), so a few hundred open vaults finish well inside 30 s on a healthy disk. WP-H4 acceptance: redeploy while a test account writes every second; the logs show the `stopped` event before the new deployment's `listening`, and the last entry written before SIGTERM is present afterwards. Schema changes ride on the existing guard: migrations run on open ("מיגרציות ממוספרות ומופעלות בכל פתיחה"), a diary from a newer version is refused with `409 server.vault.newerVersion` instead of being damaged, and a backup from a newer version with `400 server.backup.newerVersion`. Rule for releases: deploy the server **before** publishing app versions that write a newer schema; never roll the server back below the schema the volume already holds (restore from backup instead).

#### 3.6 Health check and graceful shutdown

`GET /api/health` exists (`{ ok: true, version }`, `app.ts` line 88). Hosted mode adds `uptimeSec`, `storage: 'ok' | 'degraded'`, `freePct`, `mode: 'hosted'` and keeps HTTP 200 while the process can serve reads (a restart would not fix a full disk). It is exempt from the Host check and from nothing else; it does no crypto and no disk write. The platform's deploy-time check uses it (Railway: `healthcheck.railway.app` on `PORT`, "only called at the start of the deployment", default timeout 300 s – we set `RAILWAY_HEALTHCHECK_TIMEOUT_SEC=120`; Fly: `grace_period = "10s"`) – start-up reads `accounts.json` and `sessions.json` and loads `sql-wasm.wasm`, well under a second. **`GET /api/health/strict`** (finding 15) is the external monitor's path: 503 `{ ok: false, reason: 'storage' }` when `storage !== 'ok'` or `freePct < 10`, otherwise the same 200 body – so a full or nearly full disk pages the owner even though the process is alive. Never give the strict path to the platform (it would recycle a process that a restart cannot heal). `SIGINT`/`SIGTERM`/`SIGHUP` handlers exist; the container's `CMD` is `su-exec <user> node /app/server-hosted.cjs` from `entrypoint.sh` with `exec`, so PID 1 is node and signals arrive.

#### 3.7 Sizing (measured 2026-10-17 on the owner's PC, Intel Core 5 120U, Node 24.20)

- **scrypt per login**: `DEFAULT_KDF = { N: 32768, r: 8, p: 1 }`, 64 bytes: **50–58 ms** per derivation here; memory `128·N·r` = **32 MiB** per derivation (`deriveSecret` sets `maxmem` ≥ 128 MiB). A shared cloud vCPU is 2–4× slower: budget **100–250 ms CPU and 32 MiB per sign-in**. A failed login of an unknown name costs the same (`burnDerivation`). scrypt runs on the libuv thread pool (4 threads by default), which it shares with the diary's file writes (noted in `kdf.ts`).
- **Memory per open diary**: with 120 days of demo data (122 entries, 5 habits, 370 logs; 128 KB image, 128 KB encrypted) **≈ 2.5 MB RSS per open vault**; with 730 days (727 entries, 2206 logs; 512 KB image) **≈ 3.6 MB**. An empty diary ≈ 0.4–0.6 MB. Export + encrypt of one image: 1–4 ms. Baseline of the process under tsx was 120–140 MB; the bundled `server.cjs` under plain node is expected around 70–100 MB (measure in WP-H4).
- **Vault lifetime** (`auth-service.ts`): "A session attaches on login/register or on its first authenticated request after a restart, and detaches on logout / revoke / expiry. When the last session detaches the vault is flushed and closed." Remember-me sessions live 90 days (`REMEMBER_ME_TTL_DAYS = 90`), so on a long-running server **every account that signed in with "remember me" since the last restart keeps its diary open in memory** until it logs out. With N such users: ≈ 3 MB × N. 100 users ≈ 300 MB, 300 users ≈ 1 GB.
- **Decision (WP-H3): idle-vault eviction in hosted mode** – a vault with no request for `DIARY_VAULT_IDLE_MINUTES` (default 30) whose attached sessions all carry a wrapped key (remember-me) is flushed and closed; the next request reopens it exactly as after a restart (`authenticate()`: "remember-me sessions carry the key (wrapped with the device key)"). Regular sessions cannot be evicted ("regular sessions cannot reopen a closed vault") but they expire after 12 h anyway. With eviction, memory follows **concurrently active** users.
- **Quota-sized diaries (finding 5)**: the 2.5 MB figure was measured with a 128 KB image. A diary at the quota lives in memory three times during a persist – the sql.js database (WASM heap, roughly 1–2 × the image), the exported image (`exportImage`, `vault.ts` line 216) and the encrypted copy (`encryptContainer`, line 217) – so a **10 MB** diary costs ≈ 30–50 MB while it persists and ≈ 15–25 MB at rest; with the version-1 quota of 50 MB that was ≈ 150 MB per active user and four of them would have exceeded a 512 MB instance (OOM kill = restart = lost debounce). Sizing rule with the 10 MB quota: 512 MB − ≈ 90 MB baseline − 64 MB scrypt (2 × 32 MiB) ≈ 350 MB for vaults → ≈ 10 concurrently active **quota-sized** diaries or ≈ 60–80 typical ones (≤ 1 MB). **WP-H4 measures, not estimates**: RSS of the bundled server with (a) 50 open typical vaults, (b) 5 open quota-sized vaults persisting every second, (c) a login flood at the gate's capacity; the numbers go into this section and set the instance size (512 MB or 1 GB) before go-live.
- **Recommended instance**: 512 MB RAM / shared vCPU to start (Railway usage-billed; Fly `shared-cpu-1x` 512 MB), 1 GB if measurement (b) exceeds 70 % of 512 MB. Scale vertically when the Server card shows RSS > 70 % – no code change. Account cap 300 and the quota are env values (2.1).

#### 3.8 Horizontal scaling: one instance only

State lives in the process: sessions (`SessionStore.byHash` in memory, remember-me mirrored to `sessions.json`), open vaults (`VaultRegistry`), lockout counters (`LoginLockout`), rate-limit maps, the per-account operation locks (password change / reset / delete "רצים אחד אחרי השני"). Two instances would hand out tokens the other does not know, write `accounts.json` concurrently with `rename` races, and keep two copies of a diary in memory persisting over each other. So: `replicas = 1`, Fly `--ha=false` + `count 1`, never a second volume. Coping strategy: this is a personal-diary service for hundreds, not millions – vertical scaling covers it; the day it does not, the move is a database-backed session store and a single-writer per diary, which is a different project. Restarts (finding 16): on Railway nothing restarts a crashed service by itself unless the service's **restart policy** says so – config keys `restartPolicyType: ON_FAILURE | ALWAYS | NEVER` and `restartPolicyMaxRetries` exist with no documented default, and the health endpoint is "only called at the start of the deployment" ("Railway does not monitor the healthcheck endpoint after the deployment has gone live" – docs.railway.com/guides/healthchecks). Setting: restart policy **Always**, retries unlimited (or the maximum the UI allows), in the service settings (1.4); Fly: `[[restart]] policy = "always"`. A crash loop (e.g. OOM) can still exhaust a finite retry count, so **the external uptime monitor is the real restart alarm** (3.9), and the Dockerfile sets `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=<instance − 128 MB>` so that memory pressure fails one request rather than the process. WP-H4 check: on staging, `POST /api/debug/crash` (`DIARY_DEBUG_ROUTES=1`, exits with code 2) – the service is serving again within 60 s without anyone touching the dashboard. Expected downtime per deploy or crash is seconds; a short outage shows the clients' "no connection" screen; a graceful stop loses nothing (3.5) and a hard kill loses at most the debounce window plus any write that was in back-off (`PERSIST_DEBOUNCE_MS = 250`; `RETRY_DELAY_MS = 5_000`).

#### 3.9 Monitoring and what the owner sees

- Platform dashboard: service status, deploy history, logs (no personal data by construction), CPU/RAM graphs, volume usage, backup list. Deploy failure e-mails come from the platform.
- External uptime check on **`/api/health/strict`** every 5 min (any free monitor; owner's account) → e-mail/SMS when down or degraded for > 2 checks. This is the only continuous check there is (Railway's own stops after deploy).
- Owner's Server card (in the app): version, uptime, accounts, registrations in 24 h, disk used/free, `storageOk`, RSS, last archive, last successful persist.
- Users: a server that is down shows the client's "no connection" screen with Retry (section 4b); a full disk shows a banner "Your changes are not being saved on the server" driven by `storageOk` (the mobile core already has `storage-failed` / `storage-recovered` events for the same situation; the hosted web client polls `/api/health` every 60 s while a save failed).

#### 3.10 Operator tool: deletion on request, abuse clean-up, unlock (finding 11)

Today the only way to delete an account is `POST /api/auth/delete-account` with the account's own password (`routes/auth.ts` lines 342–356 → `deleteAccount(ctx, password)`), and version 1 said "no operator UI" – yet the policy promises deletion on request, the account cap can fill with abuse, and the lockout needs a recovery path. Design:

- **Loopback admin API inside the container.** `hosted.ts` mounts `/api/admin/*` only when `req.socket.remoteAddress` is a loopback address of the *real* socket (never a header): behind the platform proxy every external connection arrives from the proxy's private address, so the only way to reach it is a shell inside the container – `railway ssh` / `fly ssh console` – which requires the owner's platform login. Any other source gets the same `404` as an unknown route (the path is not even acknowledged). Routes: `GET /api/admin/accounts` (id, username, e-mail, createdAt, lastLoginAt, diary bytes, open?, locked?), `POST /api/admin/accounts/:id/delete`, `POST /api/admin/accounts/:id/revoke-sessions`, `POST /api/admin/accounts/:id/unlock`, `GET /api/admin/status`. Deletion reuses the existing steps after the password check: a new `AuthService.adminDeleteAccount(id)` = `deleteAccountLocked` from "`this.deleting.add(account.id)`" onwards (resets, sessions, lockouts, registry record, diary files, `purgeDeletedDiaries`, listeners) under the same per-account lock; no password, no unwrapping of any key. Mail-owner release is not needed (the owner is `DIARY_OWNER_ID`), but deleting the owner's own account logs a warning.
- **`admin.cjs`** (built by `scripts/build-hosted.mjs` from `scripts/hosted/admin.ts` into `/app/admin.cjs`): `node /app/admin.cjs list | delete --username <u> | delete --email <e> | revoke --username <u> | unlock --username <u>`; it calls `http://127.0.0.1:$PORT/api/admin/...`, prints a confirmation prompt with the account's details before a delete, and never prints e-mail addresses to the platform log (stdout of an ssh session is the owner's screen only).
- **Why against the running server and not "with the server stopped"**: Railway has no stopped-but-enterable state (a stopped deployment has no container to `ssh` into), and the live process owns in-memory state (sessions, open vaults, `pendingDeletes`); editing `accounts.json` under it would race with its atomic writes.
- **Procedure (runbook, `docs/HOSTED-RUNBOOK.he.md`)**: a deletion request must come from the account's registered e-mail address (the owner replies asking for confirmation from that address if it did not); the owner runs `railway login` → `railway ssh` → the delete command → `exit` → `railway logout`; he notes the date in his own log. Abuse clean-up: `list` sorted by `createdAt`, delete the obvious throw-aways, then switch `DIARY_REGISTRATION=invite` if it continues. Unlock: `unlock --username` resets the per-account counters of 5.2.
- Tests: the admin router answers 404 from a non-loopback socket and from a loopback socket with `X-Forwarded-For` set; `adminDeleteAccount` leaves other accounts and their files untouched (reuses the existing deletion tests with a password-less path); the CLI's argument parsing.

#### 4d. Moving an existing local diary to the server and back – exact user steps and limits

#### 5.1 Threat model

#### 5.2 Brute force and credential stuffing

Existing: per-address `rateLimit(30)` on `/api/auth` (10/min per bucket in hosted mode); per-account lockout `LOGIN_LOCKOUT_MAX_FAILURES = 10` / `LOGIN_LOCKOUT_MS = 15 * 60_000`; identical answers for unknown names and wrong passwords (`401 WRONG_PASSWORD`), including cost (`burnDerivation`); password policy (`passwordProblemKey`, min length 8); confirm-step lockout for stolen tokens (`CONFIRM_LOCKOUT_PREFIX`, 10 → 15 min). **Changed for hosted mode (finding 12, design in 2.2)**: the hard lock is keyed `account:<id>@<bucket>` (10 failures from one /64 or /32 lock that bucket for 15 min), and a pure per-account counter adds a progressive delay (`min(2^(n−10), 30)` s) instead of a lock – so a stranger can slow the owner down, never shut him out; the same applies to every user. Added: the scrypt concurrency gate (5.4), the global `/api/auth` cap and a per-bucket **register** limiter. Credential stuffing (lists of e-mail+password from other breaches): one bucket gets 10 tries per account per 15 min and 10 requests per minute; a distributed attack from many /64s gets 10 tries per bucket per account, slowed by the per-account delay to about two tries a minute after the first ten, and the global cap bounds the whole – acceptable for a diary with a strong-password rule; TOTP for owners/users is WP-H10.

#### 5.3 Account enumeration

Neutral today: login, forgot-password ("תמיד אותה תשובה", with the random 0.2–1.2 s delay before any account-dependent work), reset-password (`server.reset.invalid` for every failure). Not neutral: `register` → `409 USERNAME_TAKEN` / `409 … field: "email"` (`server.auth.emailTaken`). Decision: keep the useful message (a person registering needs to know), limit it (5 registrations per hour per IP, counted on every attempt, successful or not), and document it. `GET /api/auth/accounts` is 403 in hosted mode.

#### 5.4 DoS by scrypt cost – numbers and the gate

One derivation ≈ 55 ms on the owner's laptop CPU (section 3.7); assume **≈ 150 ms on one shared cloud vCPU**. Without a gate: 30 logins/min/address × 0.15 s = 4.5 CPU-s per minute per address (≈ 7.5 % of one core) – a dozen addresses saturate the core and starve the libuv pool that also writes diaries (`deriveSecret` uses `node:crypto` scrypt on the libuv pool; `writeContainerAtomic` uses `fs.promises` on the same pool, default 4 threads – `kdf.ts` says so). **Corrected numbers (finding 10)**: on one shared vCPU four concurrent derivations do not run four times faster, they time-slice – the core delivers ≈ 6–7 derivations per second whatever the concurrency – and four busy pool threads leave none for diary persists, so version 1's "semaphore of 4 = the pool size" would have stalled every write during a login flood. Gate (WP-H2): `UV_THREADPOOL_SIZE=8` in the container environment (Dockerfile `ENV`; it must be set before Node starts) and a semaphore of **2 concurrent derivations** (`DIARY_KDF_CONCURRENCY`) with a waiting queue of at most 32; beyond that `503 server.error.busy` with `Retry-After: 2`. Six threads stay free for `fs` and the sustained CPU spent on scrypt is bounded at ≈ 2 × (0.15 / 0.15) = the two slots, i.e. a login flood takes at most the share of the core two busy threads can take, and the per-bucket login limit (10/min), the global `/api/auth` cap (300/min = 5/s, below the core's ≈ 6–7/s) and the register limiter keep the queue short for legitimate users; the health endpoint stays answerable because it does no crypto. Memory: 2 × 32 MiB = 64 MiB peak for scrypt – included in the 512 MB sizing. **WP-H4 measures on staging**: sustained successful logins per second under a flood of wrong-password requests at the global cap, and the p95 latency of a diary persist (one client writing every second) during that flood; both numbers are recorded in 3.7 and must show persists under 1 s. (Attackers who want to burn CPU cheaply will use `/api/auth/login` with random names: same cost, same limits.)

#### 5.5 Session token storage in the browser, XSS surface, CSRF

- Tokens: 32 random bytes, server stores `sha256(token)` only (`sessions.ts`), sent as `Authorization: Bearer`, kept in `localStorage` (remember-me) or `sessionStorage` – readable by any script running in the origin, so XSS is the risk that matters. Surface review: React escapes text by default; `grep -rn "dangerouslySetInnerHTML\|innerHTML" src` finds **nothing** outside tests; the UI renders user text (entries, tags, people, places, habit names, usernames) only through JSX; the server-rendered pages (`noUiPage`, `lanDisabledPage`, the 421 page) `escapeHtml` their variables; CSP `script-src 'self'` with no `unsafe-inline` for scripts (`securityHeaders`), so an injected `<script>` would not execute anyway; `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` is for inline styles only. Markdown/HTML rendering of entries: none. Exports: CSV formula-injection guard exists ("תא שמתחיל ב-`=`, `+`, `-`, `@` ... מקבל גרש מוביל"); PDF is generated server-side from text. Keep the rule in review: any future rich-text feature must sanitise.
- CSRF: no cookies, `credentials: 'omit'`, bearer header → a cross-site page cannot make the browser attach credentials; CORS allow-list denies reading answers to foreign origins; `form-action 'self'`. Not applicable.
- Clickjacking: `X-Frame-Options: DENY` + `frame-ancestors 'none'`.
- Token in URLs: never (reset tokens travel in the `#` fragment of the link, which browsers do not send to servers, and are consumed via POST body).

#### 5.6 File upload (restore) size and parsing; path handling

Restore/inspect take `{ fileBase64, fileName }` in a JSON body ≤ 25 MB, 20/min/IP, after the session check; the file is parsed by `decryptContainer` (magic, version, KDF bounds `isValidKdfParams`, `isAffordableKdf` → at most 2× the default cost), AES-GCM tag verified before anything is trusted, then `DiaryDb.fromImage` + `integrity_check` in sql.js (WASM; a malformed image cannot escape the sandbox, worst case the request fails). `fileName` is used for messages only, never as a path. All user-supplied paths (`destPath`, `srcPath`) are refused in hosted mode (2.3); the only paths the server builds are `path.join(config.usersDir, account.dbFile)` with a server-generated `dbFile`. Static serving uses `express.static` (no directory traversal; `fallthrough` to `index.html`).

#### 5.7 Secrets management on the platform; dependency updates

Secrets: `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` and `DIARY_INVITE_CODE` are **sealed** service variables that the owner types in himself (3.2) – never in the image, never in the repo (`.dockerignore` excludes `.env*`; the Dockerfile copies only the built `deploy/hosted/dist/` tree), never in an agent's output, never readable back ("Sealed variable values are not provided when using `railway variables` or `railway run`"). There is no `DIARY_SMTP_PASSWORD` variable: the SMTP password enters only through the Mail card and is stored encrypted with the device key in `server-settings.json` ("passwordEnc ... מוצפנת ב-AES-256-GCM עם מפתח המכשיר"). Agents deploy with a **project token** scoped to one environment, held in the owner's user environment (1.4); it can upload a build and read logs; it cannot open a shell, download the volume or read sealed values. A full `railway login` session can do all three – so no agent ever has one, the owner logs in only for 3.3/3.10 and logs out, and the project token is rotated (new token, old deleted) after go-live and whenever an agent session that used it is suspected of leaking it. Logs never contain secrets (existing rule). Rotation: changing `DIARY_DEVICE_KEY` invalidates remember-me and recovery keys until users sign in again (3.2) – documented as a deliberate incident response; the owner performs it (generate → edit the sealed variable → Deploy). Dependencies: production deps are `express`, `pdfkit`, `sql.js`, `bidi-js` (`server/package.json`) plus the base image. Policy: `npm audit --omit=dev` and a base-image bump (`node:24-alpine` → current patch) **monthly**, a Node LTS major move yearly; `check-no-network` runs in the build so no dependency can add outbound calls unnoticed (BANNED_DEPS list).

#### 5.8 Audit rule for `scripts/check-no-network.mjs`

Hosted mode adds **no outbound hosts except the configured SMTP** – the reason option (c) of finding 1 (an HTTPS mail API) was rejected. Concretely: (1) `server/src/hosted.ts`, `server/src/hosted/**`, `server/src/routes/admin.ts` and `server/src/routes/server-status.ts` are scanned with the default rules – no allow-list entry, so any `fetch(`, `http.request(`, `net.connect(`, `tls.connect(`, `node:https/dns/dgram/tls` import or external URL there fails the audit; the debug routes (2.2, 3.8) only read headers and call `process.exit`; (2) the hosted composition must not import `server/src/remote/**` or `mobile/core/express-shim` – enforced by a new test `server/test/hosted-import-graph.test.ts` (like the existing `mail-import-graph.test.ts`) and by a post-build check that `server-hosted.cjs` does not contain the strings `duckdns.org`, `letsencrypt.org` or `express-shim`; (3) `REMOTE_HOSTS` and `MAIL_DIR` rules stay exactly as they are; (4) the Dockerfile and `entrypoint.sh` are added to a tiny new check that they contain no `curl`/`wget` to arbitrary hosts (package installs from the base image's repositories excepted – the image is built from `node:24-alpine` plus the `su-exec` package); (4b) `scripts/hosted/admin.ts` is the one new HTTP client in the tree and may address exactly `127.0.0.1` (the audit gets a rule: in that file the only host literal allowed is `127.0.0.1`), and `scripts/hosted/restore-archive.mjs` opens no sockets at all; (5) the frontend rule `https?://(?!127.0.0.1|localhost|tauri.localhost|…)` keeps failing on hard-coded external URLs: the default server address is therefore **not** a literal in `src/` but a build-time define (`VITE_DEFAULT_SERVER_URL` → `__DEFAULT_SERVER_URL__`), set in `vite.config*.ts` from `server-default.json` at the repo root; the audit gains a rule that this define is the only place such a URL may appear, and the mobile CSP line is covered by `ALLOW_LINE` (`Content-Security-Policy`).
